\begin{frame}{Economic Interpretation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Core mechanism: a lower renewable-sector WACC reduces the user cost of capital, supports earlier renewable investment, and can temporarily lift aggregate growth during the transition.
  \item PDP8\_GF\_C shows the strongest WACC reduction and the largest near-term GDP-growth gain (2030), consistent with stronger front-loading of capital formation.
  \item PDP8\_GF\_B raises WACC relative to baseline and is associated with weaker near-term growth, indicating tighter financing conditions can delay reallocation and expansion.
  \item By 2050, GDP-growth deviations are small or slightly negative across scenarios, suggesting convergence in growth rates as level effects are absorbed and transition dynamics mature.
  \item Primary evidence in this deck comes from 5-year grouped bars: persistent WACC wedges map into persistent, but diminishing, growth differentials over successive periods.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
